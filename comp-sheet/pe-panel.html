What the numbers are. EPS 2027 = Bloomberg blended-calendar consensus for the calendar year 2027 — a fixed period, which is what makes the line a revision path rather than a rolling blend (1BF/2BF roll forward with the calendar and would mix growth into the slope). Re-rating = (1+price)÷(1+EPS revision)−1, measured between the same two weeks as the other two, so it equals the change in the card's own CY2027 P/E.
Windows are not identical. Consensus for a year that far out builds up over time: 34 of the 36 names have a CY2027 number from Jan/Feb-2025, but AGX only from Jun-2025 and AAOI from Nov-2025. Each card starts at its own first week with both a price and an estimate — flagged on the card when that is later than Feb-2025 — so a sort by revision is comparing across different windows. The 3m column in the table view is the like-for-like read.
Gaps. Weeks where BEst published no CY2027 value are left as gaps and the line spans them; the card reports how many, and carries a ⚠ when more than a third of the window is interpolated (AGX, ENR GY, 285A JP, AAOI, CIEN, COHR, CSCO, LITE, NFLX, GLW — for those the slope between points is drawn, not observed). Names whose CY2027 EPS is negative or near zero are left out of this tab (indexing to a base near zero is meaningless) — they are listed at the bottom if any.
Not on this tab (1): SPCX — no CY2027 consensus history, or the estimate is negative/near zero.

Sign convention — the trap this tab exists to avoid. Bloomberg carries capex as a negative cash outflow, so the raw BEST_CAPEX_HI is the least negative = the lowest spend: plotting it as "street hi" would draw the most frugal analyst on the tape. Everything here is in absolute spend, and the "street hi" leg is the largest-magnitude tail, filtered to be ≥ consensus, so it survives a change of sign convention and self-voids if the tails come through incoherent.
Junk-contributor guard. The tails of BEst carry garbage — ANET's capex hi printed 13.7bn against a 911mm consensus, AVGO sat eleven weeks at 17bn against 1.9bn (its total capex is ~500mm/yr). One such point flattens every other line in the card, so the street-hi leg is capped at 2.25× its own p75 (real dispersion tops out ~2.0×, junk starts ~2.45×) and any remaining tail at 2.5× the 90th percentile — which is what catches a spike in the consensus leg itself. Nothing is deleted: the axis stops and the card says how many points sit off-scale.
Units. mm or bn chosen per name off the consensus level only (≥10bn → bn), because the outlier tails would otherwise push a 1–2bn name onto a bn axis with three zeros of empty space. Each card is in the company's own reporting currency — 2330 TT in TWD, ASML NA in EUR.
capex / sales uses BEst capex and BEst sales for the same calendar year, both in millions. ⚠ For GOOG the Bloomberg revenue line is net of TAC while the house model is gross, so its intensity reads slightly high against a gross-revenue basis — a basis gap, not an error.
⚠ MSFT is the one name whose capex definition moved under the series. From FY27 Microsoft reclassifies data-centre leases from finance to operating (~$15bn/yr out of reported capex) and extended shell useful life 15→25 years. Contributors adopt that on their own schedule, so part of any MSFT capex revision here is definition, not spend — do not read its slope as a change of intent.

Why these groups and not the sector field. The coverage DB's sectors mix ORCL into "Internet/Software" and file ASML, KLAC and LRCX under "Core Tech" next to AAPL — useless for reading a supply chain. The grouping here is by role in the AI build: who spends the capex, who is the bottleneck, who designs the silicon, who fabricates it, who equips the fab, who connects the rack.
Both TSMC lines are shown (TSM the ADR, 2330 TT the Taipei line in TWD) because the P/E and the revision series are struck on different books — they are not duplicates, and the pair is a useful ADR-vs-local sanity check.
Each chart keeps its own guardrails, identical to the tab it comes from: the P/E band falls back to median + p16/p84 where a near-zero EPS blew up mean/σ; street-hi legs on the revision charts are capped at 2.25× their own p75 (junk contributors — ANET 13.7bn vs 911mm, AVGO 17bn vs 1.9bn) and any remaining tail at 2.5× the 90th percentile; capex is in absolute spend because Bloomberg carries it negative, so a raw _HI would draw the most frugal analyst. Off-scale points are counted in the chart title, never dropped silently.
Currency is each company's own reporting currency — 2330 TT in TWD, ASML NA in EUR, the rest USD. ⚠ GOOG's Bloomberg revenue is net of TAC; MSFT's capex definition changes from FY27 (finance→operating lease reclassification, ~$15bn/yr), so part of its capex revision is definitional.
