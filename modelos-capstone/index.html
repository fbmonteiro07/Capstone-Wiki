The accelerated-computing platform that sits at the center of the AI buildout — GPUs, CPUs (Grace/Vera), NVLink + Spectrum-X/InfiniBand networking, and the CUDA software moat sold as full-stack "AI factories." Data Center is ~90%+ of revenue ($75B of $82B in Q1 FY27); the rest is Gaming, Pro Viz, and Automotive/physical AI. From Q1 FY27 NVDA re-segmented Data Center into Hyperscale (~50%) and ACIE (AI clouds, industrial, enterprise, sovereign, ~50%) plus a new Edge Computing platform. Whole thesis is leverage to hyperscaler + sovereign + neocloud capex, where NVDA captures the compute, networking, and increasingly the CPU. The scale-up network is core to the moat: NVLink is ~the entire scale-up market today and NVDA is expected to remain the majority of a market growing $4B (2024) → ~$17B by 2029, 34% CAGR (MS Scale-up primer); on compute NVDA is modeled holding ~70% share into C28, with per-GW revenue economics nearly 2x its closest peer (UBS 2026 overview).
▲ Bull
the bull case rests on cheap-multiple-on-huge-EPS-growth arithmetic that shows up across three separate Barron's pieces: (1) "Rally in Chips" (Adam Levine, 2026-06-01 issue): EPS grew $0.33 (FY23) → $4.77 (FY26); Street sees $12.37 by FY28, putting the stock at 17x P/E on that basis, with a PEG <0.6x (17x P/E ÷ 0.28 PEG per Barron's table) vs the S&P 500's ~1.0x. BofA's Vivek Arya "estimates Nvidia has a durable 70% share of the AI chip market." (2) "It's Not Too Late to Buy Into Nvidia's AI Growth Story" (Al Root, 2026-05-18 issue): the stock trades at ~24x NTM earnings, a 25% discount to its own historical valuation and 5% below the SOX index — despite normally carrying a 40% premium to SOX; "since the start of 2025, Nvidia has trailed the index by more than 70 percentage points." Root's bull-case ladder: 26x avg-semi multiple → $290; reversion to historical premium-to-sector multiple → $390; midpoint $340 (a 50% gain from the then-current level). (3) The explicit risk flag in the same Root piece: Google searches for "AI bubble" are up ~400% y/y (peaking in November), and NVDA must beat sales-growth estimates of ~80% to satisfy the Street given an average beat of only ~3% over the prior 3 quarters — i.e. the search-trend/sentiment risk is rising exactly as the bar to beat stays high. Gavin Baker (CIO, Atreides, in the 2026-06-01 issue) frames the read-through skeptically across the sector: "The multiples cannot all be accurate... You have memory makers at low-to-mid-single-digit P/Es, you have Nvidia at a low P/E, you have other accelerator companies at reasonable multiples" — i.e. either NVDA is mispriced cheap or the "reasonable" multiples elsewhere (Cerebras ~280x 2027E per the same issue's Trader column, vs NVDA's sub-17x 2027 P/E) are the anomaly (Barron's, 2026-05-18 / 2026-06-01 issues).

Advantest is the #1 global automated-test-equipment (ATE) vendor — a back-end semiconductor name levered to test intensity, not wafer starts — in a duopoly with Teradyne. Two reportable segments (restated to two from Q1 FY2025): Test System Business (~90% of revenue: SoC test systems + memory test systems incl. high-performance DRAM/HBM and NAND, plus device handlers/probers) and Services & Others (~10%: support, consumables/test-interface boards, system-level test) (Advantest FY2025 results release, 2026-04-27). The structural driver is the swing of the SoC mix to AI/HPC — ~80% of SoC business is high-performance compute / AI — plus rising HBM/high-performance DRAM test intensity (Advantest Q3 FY2025 call, 2026-01-28). It is effectively the sole SoC tester at the GPU/CoWoS leaders today — CoWoS device testing "comes from Advantest," and it is "better for HPC because of very large dies" while Teradyne fits mobile/InFO (Apple); a TSMC second-source qualification of Teradyne for AI test is "very difficult" and unlikely to displace it near-term (StoneX/John Su, 2025-08-20). Customers are foundries, OSATs, IDMs and memory makers; ~98% of sales are overseas; China is ~20–25% of revenue (Q3 FY2025 call; FY2025 release).
